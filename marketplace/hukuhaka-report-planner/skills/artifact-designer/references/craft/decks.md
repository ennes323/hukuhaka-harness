## One message per slide

- The slide title is the claim, not the topic: "Route A preserves the contract", not
  "Comparison results". A reader flipping through titles alone should recover the trunk.
- Everything else on the slide supports that one claim; content that supports a different
  claim is the next slide.
- If a slide needs a paragraph to make its point, the point belongs in a document — or the
  slide is carrying two units.

## Reading distance

- A deck is read at projection distance or in an attachment skim; document body sizes fail
  here. Scale the whole ladder up (`kpi-tiles.md` hero ladder, `typography.md` hierarchy
  ladder).
- Charts on slides need direct labels and heavier strokes; a document-sized legend or tick
  set is unreadable from the back of the room. One chart per slide unless the comparison
  is between the charts.
- Practical floor: if the slide's smallest text is illegible in a page-height thumbnail,
  it will not survive projection.

## Slide anatomy

- Keep the 16:9 safe margins (`spacing.md`) identical on every slide; recurring elements
  (title block, page number, section tag) sit at identical coordinates so only content
  appears to change between slides.
- A section divider carries the section name and at most one orienting line — it is
  wayfinding, not a content slide.

## Presenting vs pre-read

- A deck spoken over holds less per slide than a deck sent to be read alone. Decide which
  it is before planning; a leave-behind hybrid needs the pre-read density with hierarchy
  strong enough to present.

## Don'ts

- Topic-label titles ("Background", "Results") — the title row is the trunk's best real estate
- A wall of bullets read aloud — the slide is the anchor's surface; speaker notes hold the script
- Shrinking content to fit — split the slide (`spacing.md`)
- A closing "Thank you" slide on a decide-job deck — end on the decision and next step
